6.1 EVM Core Variables (Mr Bhuru Nov 9 Lecture)

"EVM Brings Sanity to Software Projects"

PV
Planned Value
BCWS — Budgeted Cost of Work Scheduled to be done by today according to the Gantt plan.
EV
Earned Value
BCWP — Budgeted value of the work actually completed so far ("end value" in class notes).
AC
Actual Cost
ACWP — Actual money spent from the bank account to achieve the work completed so far.
BAC
Budget at Completion
The total original baseline budget approved for the entire project from start to finish.

Why EVM Matters (Not Too Fast, Not Too Slow)

Comparing AC vs. PV alone lies to you! Only comparing EV against both PV (schedule) and AC (cost) reveals true project health in money and time.

6.2 Variances & Performance Indexes

How Far Are We From Plan? SV, CV, SPI, CPI

SCHEDULE PERFORMANCETIME
SV = EV − PV
SPI = EV / PV
  • SV < 0 or SPI < 1: Behind schedule (e.g., SPI = 0.80 means progressing at 80% of planned rate).
  • SV > 0 or SPI > 1: Ahead of schedule.
  • Estimated Duration: Planned Duration / SPI.
COST PERFORMANCEBUDGET
CV = EV − AC
CPI = EV / AC
  • CV < 0 or CPI < 1: Over budget! (e.g., CPI = 0.889 means getting $0.89 of value per $1 spent).
  • CV > 0 or CPI > 1: Under budget (cost efficient).
  • Notice: EV is ALWAYS first in every variance & numerator in SPI/CPI!
6.3 Forecasting Formulas (Learn ALL EACs!)

Forecasting Completion: EAC, ETC, VAC, TCPI

4 EAC (ESTIMATE AT COMPLETION) FORMULAS

1. Typical Variance (DEFAULT if unspecified)

Things are off-track and will continue at current CPI efficiency:
EAC = BAC / CPI

2. Atypical Variance (One-Off Blip)

Overspend was a once-off problem; remaining work executes at planned rate:
EAC = AC + (BAC − EV)

3. Combined Cost & Schedule (CPI & SPI)

When both schedule & cost efficiency drive final cost:
EAC = AC + (BAC − EV) / (CPI × SPI)

4. Bottom-Up Re-Estimate

Original plan invalid; add new bottom-up cost:
EAC = AC + New Bottom-Up ETC

REMAINING WORK, VARIANCE & EFFICIENCY

Estimate to Complete (ETC)

ETC = EAC − AC → How much money is still left to spend from now to finish.

Variance at Completion (VAC)

VAC = BAC − EAC → At the end, did we save (+) or overrun (−)?

To-Complete Performance Index (TCPI)

TCPI = (BAC − EV) / (BAC − AC) → Efficiency required on remaining dollars to hit original BAC (>1 means harder than planned).

6.4 Mr Bhuru's Worked Example (Nov 9 Lecture)

School Portal Case: Month 5 of 10

Given Baseline Data (Customer School Portal)

Planned Duration = 10 months • Current Time = Month 5 • BAC = $400k • PV = $200k • EV = $160k • AC = $180k

TYPICAL VARIANCE
Metric Calculation Result PM Meaning (Required in Exam!)
SV & SPI 160k − 200k | 160 / 200 -$40k | 0.80 Behind schedule by $40,000 of work; progressing at 80% speed.
CV & CPI 160k − 180k | 160 / 180 -$20k | 0.889 Over budget by $20,000; earning only $0.89 value per $1 spent.
EAC (Typical) 400k / 0.8889 $450,000 Forecasted final total cost of the project.
ETC 450k − 180k $270,000 Remaining cash needed to complete the portal.
VAC 400k − 450k -$50,000 Projected to finish $50,000 over original budget.
TCPI (400−160)/(400−180) = 240/220 1.091 Must deliver $1.09 value per remaining $1 to stay inside $400k BAC.
Est. Duration 10 months / 0.80 12.5 months 2.5-month schedule overrun unless corrective action is taken.
6.5 EVM Exam Rules & PM Interpretation

Explain the Numbers Like a PM

RULE 1
Interpretation
Numbers Alone Are Half Marks
  • Mr Bhuru warns: Expect questions asking "What does that mean? What does it mean for you as a PM?"
  • Always state both the dollar/index figure AND whether the project is ahead/behind or under/over budget.
RULE 2
Typical Default
When Variance Type Isn't Stated
  • Always use EAC = BAC / CPI unless the question says the overspend was a "one-off" (Atypical) or asks to use both SPI & CPI.
RULE 3
No Guessing
Good Practice in EVM
  • "Don't speculate things" — compute directly from EV, PV, AC, and BAC earned milestones.
  • Remember: calculations sit on the Hard exam question!
