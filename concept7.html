7.1 Risk Fundamentals & Exposure (DCAP Unit 10)

Prioritizing Risk Exposure

QUANTITATIVE METRICEXPOSURE
RE = Probability × Impact
Prioritize Highest Exposure First
  • Focus on the 20% of risks causing 80% of potential impact (Pareto rule).
  • Balance high-probability/low-loss vs. low-probability/catastrophic-loss threats in the Risk Register.
INTANGIBLE RISKS100% PROB
  • Knowledge Risk: Deficient technical skills applied to a problem.
  • Relationship Risk: Ineffective collaboration or team conflict.
  • Process-Engagement Risk: Broken operational procedures reducing productivity & quality.
SOMMERVILLE CH 22RESPONSE
  • Avoidance Strategies: Reduce probability the risk occurs (e.g., use mature framework).
  • Minimization Strategies: Reduce impact if it hits (e.g., backups, cross-training).
  • Contingency Plans: Ready-to-run fallback if disaster strikes.
7.2 Prompt Lists & Qualitative Identification

Risk Categories & Identification

PROMPT LIST CATEGORIES (UNIT 10)

External

Infrastructure (networks/power), Economic (inflation/FX), Legal & Regulatory, Environmental, Political, Market, Acts of God.

Financial & Activity

Budgetary shortfalls, Fraud/Theft, Operational policy failures, Information loss, Reputational damage.

Technological & Human Resources

New/unproven innovation risks, Personnel shortfall (tracked via metrics!), Health & Safety.

6 QUALITATIVE IDENTIFICATION TECHNIQUES

Structured Discovery Tools

1. Assumptions Analysis: Stress-test every timeline assumption.
2. Checklists & Prompt Lists: Historical risk catalogs.
3. Brainstorming & Facilitated Workshops: Team risk surfacing.
4. Open-Question Interviews: Supportive, non-confrontational stakeholder interviews.

7-Step Continuous Cycle

Establish Context → Identify → Analyze → Evaluate → Treat → Monitor/Review → Communicate (never stop after one pass).

7.3 Quantitative Risk Analysis

Decision Trees, Influence & Monte Carlo

TECHNIQUE 1
Decision Trees
Branching Expected Value
  • Flowchart comparing choices (e.g., Build vs. Buy) with probabilities and payoffs at each branch.
  • Best for clear, bounded decisions; gets messy in huge systems.
TECHNIQUE 2
Influence
Causal Network Diagrams
  • Nodes hold variables/decisions showing how an upstream risk ripples across downstream schedule or cost outcomes.
TECHNIQUE 3
Monte Carlo
Probabilistic Simulation
  • Runs thousands of random trials across task duration distributions (like PERT a, m, b) to forecast completion date probabilities.
7.4 Resource Allocation (DCAP Unit 11)

Managing Finite Resources

RESOURCE SCHEDULING REALITY

Finite Resources

A PM cannot always hire more staff mid-sprint (e.g., only 1 UI designer available across parallel tasks = resource conflict).

Resource Leveling / Smoothing

Delay non-critical tasks using their Float/Slack so daily staff & equipment usage stays realistic without delaying the project end date.

4 STAGES OF RESOURCE SCHEDULING

1. Identify Resource Requirements: Skills, roles, hardware & software tools per WBS package.

2. Schedule Resources: Map allocations onto timeline & resolve overlaps.

3. Publish Resource Schedule: Communicate exact staffing commitments to the team.

4. Publish Cost Schedule: Convert labor/tool hours into phased budget curves (PV baseline).

7.5 Monitoring, Control & Reporting (DCAP Unit 12)

Visibility, Slip Charts & Control

3 PURPOSES OF PROJECT REPORTS
  • 1. Track Progress: Verify actual completion against WBS milestones.
  • 2. Detect Variance: Spot schedule/cost slippage early (EVM & Slip Charts).
  • 3. Take Corrective Action: Rebalance controls with risk (control and quality are positively correlated).
VISUAL TOOLS & MEETINGS
  • Slip Charts & Timelines: Show how target milestone dates shift across review checkpoints.
  • Status vs. Problem Meetings: Keep status reporting brief; dedicate separate focused meetings to solving blockers.
